# High School Cybersecurity · Grade 12

## Welcome

This pathway is built for students in grades 9–12 who are curious about
cybersecurity but may not feel confident yet. That is a completely normal place
to start.

You do **not** need to know how to code. You do not need a terminal. You cannot
break anything in this lab, and you can stop at any time. The activities are
designed to be tried one small step at a time.

The Grade 12 level focuses on **applied cybersecurity**: reading a public
website carefully, checking where a file came from, and explaining what you
found without exposing anyone's personal information.

## A Note for Nervous Beginners

- **Using hints is allowed.** Hints are part of learning, not a penalty.
- **You can practise without joining the competition.** Joining is optional and
  only affects the event leaderboard.
- **You can leave the competition at any time.** Your learning record stays
  yours.
- **You do not have to be fast.** The event window is for scoring; your learning
  is not a race.
- **Asking a teacher or classmate is a skill.** Collaboration is how real
  security teams work.

Before you begin, you might rate your confidence from 1 to 5. After the
activity, rate it again. The number is for you, not for a grade.

## What You Need

- A browser
- The exercise panel open in OpenCyberRange
- No terminal, no installed tools, and no local setup

Every activity in this chapter works in the lab browser. If you have a terminal
or a RangeBox, you can use it, but it is not required.

## How to Work Safely

- The DevFest portal in this exercise is **fictional**. The speakers, talks, and
  files are made up for training.
- Do not use real names, real email addresses, or real attendee information in
  your answers.
- Do not upload a lab file to a third-party website. Use the lab's browser tools.
- If you find something that looks like a real secret outside this exercise,
  stop and tell your teacher.

## The Learning Loop

1. **Look** at what the page shows.
2. **Check** where the information came from.
3. **Record** the exact page or file.
4. **Explain** what it means and what you would do next.
5. **Protect** the people whose information is involved.
