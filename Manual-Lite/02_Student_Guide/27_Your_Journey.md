# Journey & mission

**Journey & mission** starts with a different question: *who are you trying to
become?*

You choose a mission. The platform suggests practice that produces evidence for
it, and the **Living Archive** observes which tradition your verified work
begins to resemble. You are never asked to declare an archetype.

A journey is a mission, not a label.

## The page layout

The page has three parts, in this order:

1. **The Living Archive** summarises what verified work has shown so far. It
   shows the observed contributions and kinds of evidence in a separate panel
   beside the archive note, so the counts and the explanation read as one
   observation rather than an empty banner.
2. **Your current journey** shows the mission you have chosen, its summary,
   competencies, and suggested first step. The single action is **Choose
   another journey** (or **Keep this journey** while the picker is open).
3. **The journey picker** groups the available missions as cards. Each card
   shows the mission, its tradition, its competencies, and its first step. The
   card for your current mission is marked and its button is disabled.

If no journeys are available yet, the page says so instead of showing an empty
grid.

## Choose a journey

1. Open **Journey & mission** in the sidebar.
2. Read the mission for each direction. The competencies are listed underneath.
3. Choose the one that fits what you want to do.
4. Start with the **First step** shown on the card.

You can choose another journey at any time. Changing direction does not delete
your evidence, and a completed step stays completed.

## What a journey is not

- It is not a course and does not replace the catalogue.
- It is not a permanent identity or a personality test.
- It does not change your permissions, grades, or certificate rules.
- It does not appear on a public profile as a label.
- It does not rank you against anyone.

## Missions and competencies

Each journey maps a mission to existing competencies. For example, *Defend
communities* maps to incident response, detection, documentation, and network
operations; *Build security tools* maps to detection engineering, application
security, and technical writing.

The mission is what you see. The competency vocabulary stays underneath so an
instructor, an employer, or an accreditation body can read the same work in a
language they trust.

## Quests

A quest is a short, evidence-producing path — three to seven steps through
labs, scenarios, workbook answers, an artifact, a reflection, and a capstone.
Each step points to a real available activity. Completing a step links an
evidence record; it does not create a second copy.

Quest generation is deterministic and evidence-aware: it prefers a step that
fills a gap. A new quest template is reviewed like curriculum.
