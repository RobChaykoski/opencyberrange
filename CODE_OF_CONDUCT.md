# Code of Conduct

**Effective 16 November 2026**

This Code of Conduct applies to everyone who uses or contributes to the SWOCTS
Cyber Range: learners, instructors, teaching assistants, administrators,
researchers, volunteers, contractors, and contributors. It covers the platform,
labs, workbooks, repositories, issues, pull requests, chat, email, video calls,
workshops, competitions, and in-person events.

The platform teaches offensive security. That makes clear boundaries more
important, not less. Authorised practice in a lab is welcome. Unauthorised
testing, harassment, or privacy abuse is not.

## Our standards

We expect everyone to:

- treat people with respect, patience, and professional courtesy;
- assume good faith while still challenging weak evidence and unsafe claims;
- give credit for ideas, content, and work;
- protect privacy and use fictional data in labs and examples;
- make space for beginners, neurodivergent learners, disabled learners, and
  people with different levels of experience;
- accept correction and repair harm where possible;
- follow the law, the platform's acceptable-use rules, and the instructions of
  the event or course organiser.

We do not tolerate:

- harassment, intimidation, stalking, or unwanted sexual attention;
- discrimination based on age, race, ethnicity, nationality, religion, gender,
  gender identity, sexual orientation, disability, neurotype, or socioeconomic
  status;
- doxxing, sharing private contact details, or publishing another person's
  identity without consent;
- threats, sustained disruption, or personal attacks;
- sharing flags, solutions, exam answers, or assessment evidence in a way that
  defeats the learning objective;
- using the platform or its community to test, attack, or reconnoitre a system
  without written authorisation.

## Lab and security rules

These rules are part of the Code of Conduct:

1. **Only test authorised targets.** Use the lab targets provided for the
   exercise. Do not scan, exploit, or probe the platform, another learner's
   container, the host, the institution network, or the public internet.
2. **Do not use real personal data.** Labs use fictional people, `.example`
   addresses, reserved documentation ranges, and synthetic evidence. Do not
   upload real names, credentials, health data, identity documents, or private
   communications.
3. **Do not introduce live malware.** Malware samples are never uploaded to the
   public repository or a public lab. Any approved private analysis follows the
   malware-lab governance policy: isolated host, no egress, disposable storage,
   custody record, and teardown.
4. **Do not exfiltrate.** Lab data stays in the lab. Do not move flags,
   credentials, tokens, or evidence into a public repository, chat, or personal
   device except as the exercise explicitly requires.
5. **Do not attack people.** A credential, token, or account found in a lab is
   synthetic training material. Do not use it against a real service or another
   person.
6. **Report vulnerabilities responsibly.** If you find a vulnerability in the
   platform, contact `security@swocts.com` privately. Do not publish it, exploit
   it against other users, or use it to access data you are not authorised to
   see.
7. **Respect the learning environment.** Do not share live flags, full solutions,
   or assessment answers outside the intended instructor-led debrief. Explain
   the method after the exercise instead.
8. **Respect minors.** Do not contact a minor privately, request personal
   details, arrange an off-platform meeting, or share images or links that are
   not part of the approved lesson. Instructors and event staff must follow the
   institution's safeguarding process.

## Privacy and consent

- Ask before recording, photographing, or publishing someone's voice, image, or
  work.
- Use the platform's privacy controls. Do not defeat masking, consent, or
  reporting controls.
- Do not publish another learner's progress, grades, identity, or portfolio
  without consent.
- Report a privacy concern to `privacy@swocts.com`. Do not investigate another
  person's data to prove a point.

## Reporting

Report a concern to:

- **Conduct:** `conduct@swocts.com`
- **Security:** `security@swocts.com`
- **Privacy:** `privacy@swocts.com`

Include what happened, where, when, who was involved, and any evidence you can
safely share. You may report anonymously; we will act on the information we can
verify. We will keep the report confidential to the extent possible, share it
only with the people who need it to respond, and protect the reporter from
retaliation.

We aim to acknowledge a report within two business days and to agree next steps
within five business days. Urgent safety or legal concerns may be escalated
immediately.

## Enforcement

A response may include:

1. a private clarification or warning;
2. removal of a message, comment, submission, or repository access;
3. a required apology or repair action;
4. temporary suspension from a course, lab, event, or community space;
5. permanent removal from the platform or event;
6. referral to the institution, employer, or law enforcement where required.

The response should be proportionate, documented, and focused on safety. A
person who reports a concern in good faith will not be penalised for reporting.
Knowingly false or retaliatory reports are themselves a conduct issue.

## Appeals

A person subject to a significant enforcement action may request a review by
contacting `conduct@swocts.com` within ten business days. The review will be
handled by someone who was not the original decision-maker where practicable.
The review decision is final for the platform.

## Scope and review

This policy applies online and in person. It supplements, and does not replace,
institutional policies, event rules, employment obligations, or the law. Where
those are stricter, the stricter rule applies.

We review this policy at least annually and after a serious incident. Suggested
changes can be sent to `conduct@swocts.com`.
